the only sound is the squish of [overripe tomatoes hitting the dirt](https://www.ksbw.com/article/crops-left-to-die-after-central-coast-farm-faces-labor-shortage-amid-ice-fears/69803037)

Florida has been a powerhouse of winter produce

Hundreds of farms across the Sunshine State are facing a [worker shortage so deep that it is forcing some to shut their gates](https://www.hortidaily.com/article/9209322/florida-growers-leave-behind-unharvested-crops/) for good.

From the strawberry patches of Plant City to the tomato groves of Homestead, crops are being left to rot because there simply are not enough people to pick them.

In Central Florida’s strawberry belt, one grower slashed output to 35 percent of normal this season, unable to replace workers spooked by deportation raids.

[Half his usual crew lacked papers; the rest came via H-2A visas, but fear kept numbers low](https://www.politico.com/news/2025/02/06/desantis-florida-agriculture-undocumented-workers-00202843).

agricultural jobs dropped 155,000 nationwide in recent months, the sharpest fall in a decade

[For generations, the rhythm of Florida’s agriculture has been set by seasonal hands](https://blogs.ifas.ufl.edu/gcrec/2020/03/25/the-changing-faces-of-florida-farm-workers/).

Farmers have long reported that domestic workers are largely unwilling to take these jobs, creating a reliance on [immigrant labor](https://guides.uflib.ufl.edu/c.php?g=985707&p=8685436#:~:text=Archiving%20Farmworkers%20Histories%20at%20UF,Farmworker%20Communities'%20Histories%2D%20The%20Source).

According to reports, about 70% of farmworkers in the U.S. are foreign-born, and roughly 40% of those are undocumented

In Florida, the new immigration law signed by Florida’s Governor has created uncertainty among farmworkers

The Florida Policy Institute estimated that this change could [cost the state economy more than $12 billion](https://www.floridapolicy.org/posts/florida-hb-1617-sb-1718-potential-economic-and-fiscal-impact#:~:text=2,3) in just one year

The labor shortage is having a [devastating impact on small and mid-sized farms](https://foodprint.org/blog/how-the-current-immigration-crackdown-is-impacting-food-and-farmworkers/#:~:text=Raids%20have%20also%20had%20a,crops%20in%20areas%20like%20Florida.)

Farmers are struggling to maintain their operations, with some reporting that up to 70% of their workforce has failed to show up, fearing immigration enforcement actions.

Small and mid-sized family farms are hitting a wall. They cannot compete with massive corporate operations or with cheap produce being shipped in from Mexico

In Polk County alone, one tomato farmer reported losing $800,000 in a single month because his produce sat unpicked.

When a family farm that has been around for 50 years closes, it takes a piece of the community with it

Local trucking companies, packing houses, and equipment sellers all feel the pinch when the harvest fails.

State leaders have suggested various solutions, from recruiting domestic workers to [expanding the H-2A temporary agricultural worker visa program](https://www.farmers.gov/working-with-us/h2a-visa-program).

Many smaller farmers find the program too burdensome or expensive to navigate effectively.
