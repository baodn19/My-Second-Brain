# 1st Pass: Extract information into headings: research question, problems solved, contributions, methodologies, and limitations
## Research question
- What is the equilibrium impact of industrial robots on local US labor markets, specifically regarding employment and wages?
## Assumption
- Commuting zones housing industries with greater advances in robotics technology are not differentially affected by other labor market shocks or trends
## Problems
- Existing literature lacks systematic evidence on the equilibrium effects of automation and robots on employment and wages.
- Prior approaches predicting job susceptibility do not account for cross-sector equilibrium responses.
- The empirical strategy solves the identification problem of isolating technological advances from idiosyncratic US economic shocks using international data.
## Contributions
- Develops a theoretical framework demonstrating how robots impact labor demand through distinct displacement and productivity effects.
- Provides robust empirical evidence that industrial robots negatively affect employment and wages across US commuting zones.
- Estimates that one additional robot per thousand workers locally reduces the employment-to-population ratio by 0.39 percentage points and wages by 0.77%.
- Calculates aggregate national effects of a 0.2 percentage point drop in employment and a 0.42% drop in wages per one additional robot per thousand workers.
- Demonstrates that the labor market impact of robots is fundamentally distinct from overall capital deepening and IT technology investments.
## Limitations
- Data from the International Federation of Robotics (IFR) contains unclassified robots and lacks early industry breakdowns for the US.
- Extrapolating aggregate estimates relies heavily on specific structural model assumptions regarding cross-commuting zone spillovers, trade, and capital markets.
- The findings may not capture slowly emerging general equilibrium effects or shifts in employment responses as robots become highly widespread over time.

# 2nd pass
## Methodology
- Constructs a [[Bartik-style]] measure of local exposure to robots using baseline industry employment shares combined with industry-level robotics advances.
- Employs an Instrumental Variable (IV) strategy using robot adoption trends in advanced European economies to instrument for US exposure.
- Estimates local effects using long-differences and stacked-differences regressions across commuting zones.
- Uses a structural model of trade between commuting zones to quantify the aggregate effects of robot adoption while accounting for economic spillovers.
