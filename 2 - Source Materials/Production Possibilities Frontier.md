- *Definition:* the [[Opportunity Costs]] an economy faces in the production of two goods.
- *Characteristics:*
	- Snapshot in time of production
	- PPF curve is **bowed out** = Different resources are better for different good
	$\implies$ There is an initial low opportunity cost for reducing good Y to increase good X.
	$\implies$ After a certain point, the opportunity cost increase since nonoptimal resource is being used to produce good X.
- *Example:*

|                  | A   | B    | C    | D    | E    |
| ---------------- | --- | ---- | ---- | ---- | ---- |
| Boots            | 0   | 20   | 40   | 60   | 80   |
| Jackets          | 100 | 90   | 70   | 40   | 0    |
| $\Delta$ Boots   |     | + 20 | + 20 | + 20 | + 20 |
| $\Delta$ Jackets |     | - 10 | - 20 | - 30 | - 40 |
- *Change of O.C.:*
	- **RISE** as give up more of the good that is **SCARCE**
	- **LOWER** when the good is in relative **ABUNDANCE**
- *Shift in PPF:* happens when there is a change in resource
	- **Bias:** a shortage in rubber, this affects the production of boots relatively more than the production of jackets
	- **Equal:** a shortage in worker
