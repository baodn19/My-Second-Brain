# 1st Pass: Extract information into headings: research question, problems solved, contributions, methodologies, and limitations
## Research question
- Does the adoption of industrial robots create a trade-off between productivity gains and employment losses in the manufacturing sector of a [[developing country]]?
## Problems
- The paper addresses the lack of empirical evidence regarding the impact of automation in developing countries at early stages of industrialization.  
- It explains the mechanism behind differing employment outcomes between advanced and developing economies by analyzing the role of diminishing productivity returns to robot adoption.

## Contributions
- The study documents that the aggregate local employment impact of industrial robots in the manufacturing sector is positive in a developing country context, specifically Indonesia.
- It provides plant-level evidence demonstrating that this absence of an employment-productivity trade-off is caused by diminishing productivity returns to automation.
- The findings challenge the concept that capital-intensive technologies explain the poor employment performance of manufacturing in countries at early stages of industrialization.

## Limitations
- The actual adoption of robots is not directly observed at the plant level.
- Plant-level exposure to automation is proxied by the share of secondary education workers, relying on the assumption that these individuals primarily perform routine manual tasks suitable for automation.
- The local labor market analysis requires the assumption that labor mobility across Indonesian administrative regencies is limited.
- The identification strategy assumes that shocks to global automation possibilities only affect domestic employment trends through their direct impact on robot use in Indonesia.

# 2nd pass
## Methodology
- The researchers employ a Bartik-style shift-share approach to measure local labor market exposure to automation, interacting baseline industry employment shares with industry-specific robot imports.
- An instrumental variable strategy (2SLS) is utilized to address endogeneity, using average industry-specific robot penetration in OECD countries to instrument for Indonesian robot adoption.
- For the plant-level analysis, exposure to robots is measured by interacting industry-level robot imports with a plant's base-year share of secondary education workers.
- Plant-level productivity is measured by estimating translog production functions using a control function approach to obtain quantity-total factor productivity.
