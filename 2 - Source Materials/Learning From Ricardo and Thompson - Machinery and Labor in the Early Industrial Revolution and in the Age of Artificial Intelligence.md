# 1st Pass: Extract information into headings: research question, problems solved, contributions, and limitations
## Research question
- What factors caused [[David Ricardo]] to revise his initial belief that machinery would benefit workers?
- How do historical lessons from the early [[industrial revolutions]] apply to the potential impact of [[artificial intelligence]] on labor today?

## Problems
- **Challenging the Productivity Bandwagon**: Addresses the long-held economic view that total productivity increases always lead to higher wages and shared prosperity.
- **The Weaving Paradox**: Explains why real wages for handloom weavers collapsed for decades even as cotton textiles became a dominant, high-growth sector of the British economy.
- **Integrating Economic and Social Factors**: Bridges Ricardo’s focus on marginal productivity with E.P. Thompson’s emphasis on workplace power, autonomy, and monitoring.

## Contributions
- **Empirical Evidence**: Documents that real wages for handloom weavers more than halved between 1806 and 1820, likely declining to 25% of their peak "golden age" level.
- **Mechanisms of Wage Growth**: Identifies that automation benefits labor only when it is coupled with the creation of new tasks that raise labor's marginal productivity or when there is sufficient hiring in complementary industries.
- **Political Economy Insights**: Highlights that workers fail to benefit from productivity gains when they lack political voice and the legal right to bargain collectively. $\to$ Is union a solution?
- **AI Policy Implications**: Suggests that if AI is used primarily for automation and surveillance rather than creating new tasks, it will likely depress labor demand and shift the balance of power from workers to managers.

## Limitations
- **Historical Data Quality**: Acknowledges that available data from the early 19th century does not meet modern standards of quality.
- **Index Debates**: Notes ongoing academic disagreement regarding the most accurate consumer price indices for the period (specifically the [[Allen vs. Clark debate]]).
- **Precise Timing**: Indicates that the exact timing of when economy-wide real wages turned upward in the 1830s remains difficult to discern.
