# Foreword
- *Idea 1:* Startups are about finding customers, not building products.
- *Idea 2:* People don't buy products or services; they buy solutions to their problems.
- *Idea 3:* Entrepreneurs are detectives, not fortunetellers.
- *Idea 4:* Successful entrepreneurs are luck makers, not risk takers; making small bets that they can afford to lose to test assumptions and find new opportunities.
# Chapter 8: Put Your Customers and Their Needs before
- *The purpose of any companies:* "the way to start a successful company, is to solve problems that your customers are already experiencing rather than try to convince them that a problem exists." (pg. 41)
- *Any metrics apart from sales can be misleading:* "vanity metrics - the press coverage, the site visits, all that stuff - it all makes you feel good, but it doesn't really measure anything useful. It doesn't really depict the health of your company" (pg. 40)

# Chapter 9: Don't Gamble - Use Small Bets
- *Make calculated bets instead of "all-in":* 
	- "winning at this tournament wasn't about luck, it was about reducing the number of times she had to get lucky" (pg. 50)
	- "They are taking greater and greater bets to try to recoup their investment, They are now hoping to get lucky and, ironically, will just end up spiraling to a faster death" (Pg. 77)
# Chapter 12: Only Customers Can Tell You if You've Found a Problem Worth Solving
- *Initiating with small talk shows that you care about the people:* 
	- "Sam was happy to talk business all night, but she was hoping for a few minutes to enjoy her angel hair pasta." (Pg. 64)
	- "Don't be all business. The more conversational you can be, the more they will share." (Pg. 167)
- *Don't try to sell the products when you're learning about the customers:* "You have to take yourself out of sales mode when trying to identify your customer and their problem. Your objective should be to learn, like you are a detective."
- *Finding the migraine-level problem that people spent time and money on:* 
	- "no selling , just learning, no talking about any solution, just trying to figure out the problem, and whether its a headache or a migraine." (Pg. 71)
	- "The bigger the pain, the easier it will be to sell the solution" (Pg. 73)
	- "If you spend time trying to gather intelligence, every now and then, you'll get lucky and strike gold!" (Pg. 155)
	- "Try to get the potential customer to articulate their pain without leading them in any way" (Pg. 166)
# Chapter 16: The Only Way to Get Good at Customer Interviews Is to Practice
- *Avoid binary questions:* "Don't use 'would you'! Those are the worst two words you can start a sentence with! Don't ask yes or no questions. Make sure you are giving them plenty of opportunities to tell their story." (Pg. 97)
# Chapter 18: Don't Pivot to a New Idea without Testing Your New Assumptions
- *Maintain your support system:* "Owen was felling sleepy. But he knew this was one of the most important interviews he'd conduct all day." (Pg. 115 - Owen talking to his wife about tea at her workplace)
- *Test assumptions with as little resource as possible:* "spend the least amount of resources figuring out if your assumptions are right or wrong" (Pg. 171)
# Chapter 20: Successful Entrepreneurs Recognize Failure, Fold, and Live to Fight Another Day
- *The purpose of a startup:* "a startup is just a temporary vehicle to find a good business model. Not just a problem and a solution, but an entire business model. That includes what kind of sales force you are going to use and how much they will cost, how you are going to get your supplies, how much your expenses are going to be, and finally, how much revenue you could possible make from this business." (Pg. 130)
- *Admit failure to prevent further cost and continue to play:* "What kind of poker player would you be if you never folded a hand? Your chips would run out in a hurry. And once you fold a hand, you can look forward to playing the next one. You have to play a lot of hands before you find an opportunity to win a big pot." (Pg. 132)
# Chapter 26: The Best Feedback from Potential Customer
- *Talk when people sit down:* "People sitting down are more likely to talk and endure questions than people walking or standing. It's just harder for them to get away" (Pg. 164)
- *Always offer sth before asking for a favor:* "A few minutes later Sam came back from the faux Bier Garden Place and positioned a beer in front of Brian. "Here. All you gotta do is answer a few questions for me."" (Pg. 165)
- *Always ask for referral:* "Always ask a potential customer if they know anyone else you could interview"

# Template:
- *Define the business:* I will help ... (customers) solve ... (problem) by ... (solution).
- *Confirm a problem worth solving:*
	1. My customers are ...
	2. Their problem is ...
	3. They are currently solving their problem by ...
	4. They have tried other solutions in the past by ...
	5. On a scale of 1-10, the seriousness of the problem is ...
	6. They would spend $... to fix this problem.
